Market DEFINITION

The market for generative AI software development and enterprise AI platform services that build AI-powered business process automation for businesses, sold internationally with a focus on Western technology markets. ➜ The buyer is an SME CEO, a buy-and-build fund partner or a corporate development lead who is purchasing an always-on screening and memo-generation service that replaces the manual long-list, the banker teaser and the associate's spreadsheet with a nightly agent that returns qualified targets and written investment memos.[1][2] The structural friction is that no one inside these organisations is paid to source - the CEO does it between client meetings, the associate does it between live deals, and the banker only shows what is already for sale, so the market of roughly 3,000 relevant companies per buyer is never actually screened.

In the value chain, upstream sits the raw signal layer (deal announcements, fundraising data, company registries) which is commoditised, downstream sits the decision and the transaction which is where the profit pool concentrates, and the middle layer - screening, qualification and memo writing - is exactly where Proplace positions itself, which is a structurally squeezed position unless the reasoning layer compounds into something the buyer cannot rebuild.

Our Market THESIS

The irreversible break is that agentic AI has moved from demonstration to production inside financial workflows, with agentic AI expanding faster than generative AI from a smaller base, and the EU AI Act now imposing conformity, documentation and registration obligations on providers and deployers of AI systems - which means governed, auditable AI sourcing tools are becoming a procurement requirement rather than a novelty.[3][4] The sourced competitive data does not allow us to claim that incumbents are structurally unable to respond: IBM, Accenture, Deloitte and Infosys all sell AI software development and enterprise AI platform services, and the sourced set shows no named incumbent offering a dedicated always-on M&A sourcing agent for SMEs, so the honest statement is that the incumbent paralysis thesis is unproven rather than established.[5][6] The attack vector is the SME and mid-cap buyer who has no corporate development team, no banker mandate and no associate to spare - a segment the systems integrators cannot serve profitably at a 25,000 EUR annual price point and the data providers do not serve at all.

The window is open now because European SME M&A is fragmenting into thousands of sub-scale targets that no banker will shop, and it closes when either a data incumbent bundles screening into an existing terminal subscription or the systems integrators productise a competing agent - implied timing of 24 to 36 months.

Company Deep Dive

Mind map — what Proplace does

Proplace develops Stan, an AI agent dedicated to corporate development, which automates the discovery, qualification, and financial analysis of acquisition or investment targets. The platform targets CEOs of high-growth SMEs, buy-and-build funds, strategic acquirers, and investors (VC, family offices) in Europe, eliminating 94% of manual sourcing work.

Stan derives acquisition criteria from the buyer's website and thesis, then daily scans a market of ~3,000 companies. It discards irrelevant targets with a written justification and proposes 5 to 10 board-ready acquisition or investment decisions per month, each accompanied by a detailed investment memorandum. A human corporate developer refines Stan to replicate the buyer's reasoning.

The business model is based on an annual SaaS subscription, with prices ranging from EUR 25,000 to EUR 50,000 per year, including a free trial. The team, led by Alexandre Guillaume, consists of experts with capital deployment experience at Partech and Ardian, and aims to become the default corporate development AI layer for European SMEs and funds.
